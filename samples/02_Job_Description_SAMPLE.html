Propulsion Engineer

Hybrid, distributed; test work onsite · $115,000-$155,000 base salary + equity

We build connected hardware, and the propulsion team is small: a handful of engineers and one hardware partner. You would own a propulsion subsystem, not a slice of one.

The work is hands-on in the way propulsion always is. You will spend more time on what the hardware does on the test stand than on a clean analysis deck. Hot-fires fail, sensors lie, and someone has to find out why before the next build. That is the job, and it is also why people here stay.

What you'll own
  • ·Design and analyze a propulsion subsystem end to end: what it must do, how it is sized, how it is verified.
  • ·Own hot-fire and thrust-stand test: build the setup, instrument it, run it, and reduce the data into decisions.
  • ·Thermofluids and combustion, or electric-propulsion analysis: CFD, FEA, and thermal models the hardware then confirms or refutes.
  • ·Work the interfaces: feed system, structures, avionics, and the one external hardware partner. A few weeks a year on console through test campaigns.
What we need
  • ·Propulsion system design in at least one type: liquid, solid, hybrid, or electric. You have taken a design from requirements to real hardware.
  • ·Real command of thermofluids: thermodynamics, fluid dynamics, and heat transfer applied to high-energy hardware in production or test settings.
  • ·Hands-on propulsion test: hot-fire or thrust-stand work, test-stand instrumentation, and turning raw runs into clean data reduction.
  • ·CAD plus analysis fluency: CFD, FEA, or thermal modeling in a standard toolchain, for example ANSYS, Siemens NX, or SolidWorks.
  • ·You have owned a subsystem end to end on flight or test hardware, including the parts nobody scoped, from requirements through verification.
Direct space-propulsion experience is not required and is not an edge in our screen. The same problem, controlled high-energy fluid and thermal systems on flight-critical hardware, shows up in gas-turbine and jet-engine work, automotive and motorsport powertrain, and high-pressure process systems. We have hired from those fields, and those engineers are among our strongest. If that is you, apply.

Base range reflects the posted location. Compensation also includes equity, health coverage, retirement plan, and paid time off. Open until filled.

We are an equal opportunity employer. All qualified applicants will be considered without regard to any characteristic protected by applicable law. If you need an accommodation to apply or interview, tell your recruiter.

Opening
Team size and the hardware partner are in the first two lines. For a small propulsion team, "own a subsystem, not a slice" is the strongest promise in the post.
Honest cost
The test-stand line is deliberate. Naming that hot-fire and hardware work is hands-on and mostly onsite raises reply quality and kills a late-stage surprise.
Five, not twelve
Long requirement lists suppress applications, including from the adjacent-industry engineers we most want. Everything cuttable was cut to five.
No eligibility gate
This search ships no work-authorization or clearance line. Propulsion often carries one in the wider market; this role does not, so none was invented or implied.
The closer
The last paragraph converts adjacent-industry engineers, powertrain, gas-turbine, motorsport, who would otherwise self-reject. It is the highest-yield sentence in the file.
