01
The read

The constraint is mid-career reach, not headcount

About 68,000 engineers work nationally in the base occupation, but the mid-career, field-proven battery subset is only a few thousand, and it clusters in a handful of metros. EV makers, funded storage startups, and large cell manufacturers all chase the same people. You will not win this on base salary. You win on ownership scope, test cadence, and adjacent pools nobody else is fishing.

Insight

Adjacent-industry engineers, EV powertrain, power electronics, and consumer electronics, close the home-storage gap in months, not years. That reframe materially widens the reachable pool, and title-based searches rarely surface these people for a battery role.

Caution

Battery talent is concentrated in a few clusters, so most hires need relocation. A distributed posting will not change that: test-rig and abuse-test work is onsite by nature. Budget relocation now, or the search stalls on geography alone.

Procedure

Work-authorization requirements, where a role has them, are a role constraint, not a sourcing filter. Ask the identical question of every candidate, in writing, as procedure. Never infer eligibility from a profile, a name, or a school.

How to read this, and what to do

The Bay Area is your anchor. It holds the largest renter-heavy pool, so many hires need no move at all, plus the deepest battery identity in the country. Seattle is the strongest relocation source next, a mobile, no-income-tax metro with an established clean-energy base. Both beat the rooted, high-ownership clusters on pool quality per dollar of relocation spend.

Detroit and Reno look strong on pool and cost, and fail on propensity.Historical relocation-acceptance from these metros runs low for this role family, so package alone rarely closes them. Source them for exceptional inbound only, and do not spend a sourcing block chasing relocations there.

Working sequence 1 · Bay Area → 2 · Seattle → 3 · Denver, Austin, Boston · Skip Detroit / Reno

Personas

Four people you will actually talk to

Each persona needs a different opening line. The risk line is what to probe at the screen, not a reason to pass.

The field-proven builder

Mid engineer, 4 to 6 years on shipped or test-validated packs.

Optimizing for
Ownership and shipping hardware, a full subsystem rather than a slice
Message key
"Own a full battery subsystem here, not a slice of someone else's."
Risk
May expect big-team resources. Probe for scrappiness on a small team.
The adjacent-industry crossover

EV, power-electronics, or consumer-electronics engineer eyeing a move into home storage.

Optimizing for
A mission and a real step up in impact over their current field
Message key
"Your thermal and electrical skills transfer directly, and here they serve a mission."
Risk
Ramp on home-storage safety standards. Screen for adaptability.
The test-and-analysis specialist

Battery test or thermal analyst who wants design ownership.

Optimizing for
Growth from analysis into hands-on design ownership
Message key
"Design-and-test ownership here, not analysis handed off to someone else."
Risk
Unproven hands-on design depth. Probe for real build experience.
The early-career high-slope

Two to three years, strong fundamentals, steep growth trajectory.

Optimizing for
Learning speed and scope beyond the years of experience
Message key
"Outsized responsibility early, on a team small enough to see everything."
Risk
Mentorship load on a small team. Weigh the ramp cost honestly.
