The loop

Five stages, cheapest cut first

Ordered so the cheapest cut comes first. Nobody spends four hours on a test-rig problem to fail on something the thirty-minute screen would have surfaced.

03
Questions

Questions with the bar written down

A question with no written bar is a vibe check. Each card says what a four sounds like and what a two sounds like, so two interviewers score the same answer the same way.

Tech 2 · battery test anomaly
20 min
"A cycling run shows one module six degrees hotter than its neighbours, but the cell voltages look nominal. Walk me through what you rule out, in order."
Scores 4
Separates measurement from physics: checks thermocouple placement and rig calibration before touching the design. Asks what changed since the last good run. Names what would disprove each theory.
Scores 2
Blames the cooling plate at once and redesigns it. Trusts the temperature reading without checking the rig or the sensors. Cannot say what evidence would change their mind.
Tech 2 · thermal-system design tradeoff
25 min
"Design a pack cooling loop that still keeps every cell in its safe window if one pump fails. What do you cut to ship it in a week, and why is that cut safe?"
Scores 4
Names the failure mode first, offers two real architectures with a stated tradeoff, and says exactly what they cut under the week and why it stays safe for this failure.
Scores 2
Proposes one architecture with no fallback, cannot say what actually fails for the product when the pump stops, and needs the interviewer to name the tradeoff first.
HM · ownership on a small team
15 min
"Tell me about a battery problem you owned end to end that nobody assigned you. What made it worth the effort, and what did it cost?"
Scores 4
A specific gap they caught before anyone flagged it, the real cost of ignoring it, the actual sequence of what they did, and who they had to convince.
Scores 2
Describes assigned test or analysis work as if it were initiative. No cost named, no tradeoff, no other people in the story, and no reflection.
Sys · works with cell partner
15 min
"Describe a time your analysis said the hardware was wrong and the partner disagreed. How did you settle it?"
Scores 4
Built test or analysis evidence before making the claim, treats the partner as competent, and reports what happened after, including where they turned out partly wrong.
Scores 2
The story is about being right. The cell partner is the antagonist. No evidence step, and no sign they updated when new data arrived.
04
Debrief

The debrief, and the two hard-noes

Scores are written before the room opens. The order exists to stop the most senior or the loudest voice from anchoring everyone else.

Run it in this order
1
Scores written and submitted first
No score, no vote. Whoever has not written their scorecard does not speak first.
2
Lowest scorer opens the room
Inverts seniority on purpose, so concerns get air before the loudest enthusiasm anchors everyone.
3
Owners read one row each
Follow the heat grid. A three on a hard senior question is not a three on an easy one.
4
Calibrate, do not average
Weight for hawk-or-dove interviewers, early-round nerves, and format mismatch before comparing scores.
5
Decide inside the hour
Any single one forces extended discussion. Then hire, no-hire, or one named missing check with an owner.
Gate: hard no

Cannot reason about a battery pack as one coupled thermal, electrical, and mechanical problem. After two stages of prompting they still size a component in isolation. On a small team shipping hardware into homes, that is the expensive miss.

Gate: hard no

Treats the cell partner as an obstacle rather than a peer, and cannot name a test result that once changed their mind. Unworkable on a team this small.

Not a gate

No big-automaker pedigree. No master's degree. No one specific analysis package. None of these predict battery judgment, and every one of them shrinks an already small pool.
