Timing

Why the demand curve favors moving now

These are structural, publicly disclosed forces, not a rumored competitor event. Demand for battery talent is climbing across three fronts while the qualified pool stays small, which is the whole argument for moving fast on the SLA.

Window · home and grid storage demand

Home and grid storage have added tens of thousands of jobs across EVs, utilities, and consumer hardware. Demand for battery engineers is outpacing a pool that grows slowly, so every credible candidate is being courted by several employers at once (industry 2025-26).

Window · new battery frontiers

Sodium-ion cells, second-life packs, and bidirectional charging are pulling on the same talent, while fast-charge thermal design and solid-state cells are the hot specialties. New programs open faster than schools graduate battery engineers (industry 2026).

Your edge · mid-career shortage

The scarcity concentrates at 5-15 years with real shipped-pack experience, which is exactly the band this search targets. A small team offering hands-on hardware ownership is a genuine differentiator for that engineer. Move while that pitch is fresh (industry 2025).

E
Question bank

Ask these, in this order

Each question extracts one thing you cannot source without. The note says what it pulls out and what a bad answer tells you: a vague answer here reliably predicts a vague battery search.

"What does success look like in the first 90 days, and what will this engineer have designed, analyzed, or tested by the end of year one?"
Extracts Concrete scope for the posting and the interview scenarios: which hardware, which tests, which analysis. A vague answer here predicts a vague search, so push twice before moving on.
"Which component or subsystem will they own outright on day one: thermal system, module design, test rig? Who owns it today?"
Extracts Ownership scope, plus whether this is a backfill or growth seat. Candidates ask this on the first call, so you need the answer before they do.
"Who was your last great battery hire, and what specifically made them exceptional in their first six months?"
Extracts The real bar, in behavioral terms. Converts "strong engineer" into observable evidence you can screen for.
"Walk me through the team: who do they report to, how do they work with external suppliers, will this role ever have direct reports?"
Extracts Structure for the posting and the candidate FAQ. Also surfaces the supplier-coordination load before a candidate discovers it at onsite.
"What is the hardest battery problem the team is staring at this quarter: thermal runaway margin, cell-to-cell imbalance, an abuse-test anomaly?"
Extracts Interview deep-dive material and the single best outreach hook for experienced engineers. Get it verbatim: paraphrasing kills it.
"A candidate is excellent in LFP packs but has only dabbled in NMC. Screen out, or screen in and train?"
Extracts Whether the chemistry requirement is real or inherited. Confirm the answer still holds when you present an actual candidate.
"They have deep thermal and test experience from EV powertrain, power electronics, or consumer electronics, but no home-storage heritage. Yes or no?"
Extracts The adjacent-industry boundary. This one answer roughly doubles or halves the reachable pool.
"Which requirement would you drop to close two weeks faster?"
Extracts Any answer is useful. "None" means the timeline is not real, and you should say so in the room rather than discovering it in week seven.
"What would make you pass on an otherwise technically excellent battery candidate?"
Extracts Real disqualifiers, and often an unstated bias you can address now rather than at debrief.
"What is the unglamorous part of this job you would rather I not mention: night test-rig shifts, supplier travel, certification paperwork?"
Extracts The cause of your late-stage dropouts. Put it in the posting instead: naming it raises reply quality and kills the offer-stage surprise.
"Give me your sixty-second pitch for this role: impact, hardest challenge, why now."
Extracts Authentic selling language in their voice. Record it. It goes straight into outreach and the screen close.
"Can you commit to scorecard feedback within two business days, and a named panel this week?"
Extracts The SLA. In a scarce, concentrated pool, hiring-manager latency is the single largest controllable loss factor. Ask for the commitment out loud.

Requirement calibration

Each documented requirement, checked against a small, concentrated battery pool. Bring this table to the meeting already filled in with your recommendation: it is far easier to get a verdict on a proposal than on an open question.

Requirement, as documented Verdict Market check & recommendation
Battery pack design (LFP, NMC, or another lithium-ion family) Must-have Hold. This is the core signal and the deep-dive is built to verify it. Defining the search around it, not one specific chemistry, keeps the small pool open.
Thermal and electrical: heat transfer, circuits, cell behaviour Must-have Hold. The physics under every pack decision, and cheap to verify at screen. No pool cost: any real candidate has it.
Cell behaviour and safety: aging, thermal runaway, protection Calibrated Hold, but read it as either/or across chemistries. Requiring one specific chemistry narrows an already small pool sharply for little gain.
Battery test: cycling / abuse test, instrumentation, data reduction Calibrated Hold as must-have, but accept adjacent test-rig experience: EV validation labs, power-electronics test benches, consumer-electronics qualification. This is where the peripheral pools open up.
CAD plus analysis: thermal, FEA, electrical (ANSYS, MATLAB, SolidWorks) Flex Require the analysis skill, not the named package. A strong analyst learns a new tool in weeks; screening on the specific suite cuts the funnel for no real gain.
Systems engineering on shipped hardware: requirements, tolerances, verification Flex Field heritage specifically is scarce. Accept rigorous hardware-qualification experience from EV, power electronics, consumer electronics, or e-mobility, and ramp on home-storage standards.
Thermal-interface materials / cell welding Nice-to-have Tiebreaker only. Valuable for module and busbar work, but rare enough that requiring it would empty the pool. Make sure sourcers are not hard-filtering on it.
BMS logic; MATLAB or Python; UL 9540 process Nice-to-have All trainable on the job. Treat as a plus that speeds ramp, never a filter. Process fluency in particular is learned faster than battery physics.

The trade-off lock and a mutual SLA

Every search optimizes two of three. Make the choice explicit and record it, then commit both sides in writing. Send this as the meeting recap the same day: a battery search that slips almost always slips on the right-hand column.

Pick two: speed · bar · comp
Speed + Bar
Comp lands at the top of the band. Approve the ceiling up front, not at offer, or the finalist walks to a better-funded program.
Speed + Comp
The bar flexes on home-storage heritage. Hire strong thermal and test talent from an adjacent industry, and commit to a real ramp plan.
Bar + Comp
Timeline extends past the market average. Each extra month of vacancy is a month of design and abuse-test work not getting done. Sometimes correct, but say it out loud.
