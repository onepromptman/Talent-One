01
The premise

Outbound carries a scarce specialist pool

This propulsion search is scarce and concentrated before any filtering happens. Inbound alone will not clear a mid-career hire out of a pool this small. Everything here assumes the recruiter builds pipeline directly, in flight programs and in the adjacent industries solving the same combustion and thermofluids problem under a different name.

5×
More likely to land the job than an inbound applicant
Sourced candidates convert at about 2% application-to-hire versus roughly 0.4% for inbound applicants, and clear the initial screen far more often: about 44% versus 6%.

02
Strings

Search strings for a concentrated pool

Copy-paste ready, filters and all. Each card states expected volume and the pool it deliberately misses, because naming the blind spot tells the recruiter which string to run next and which one only sizes the market. Volume estimates are order-of-magnitude reads from platform result counts, not a census.

A · BASIC (broad pool)
Est. ~2,500 national · ~450 in target metros
("Propulsion Engineer" OR "Propulsion Systems Engineer" OR "Rocket Propulsion Engineer" OR "Liquid Propulsion Engineer" OR "Electric Propulsion Engineer" OR "Propulsion Test Engineer") AND (propulsion OR "rocket engine" OR thruster OR "hot-fire")
Filters: US and Canada · 3+ years total experience
Use for: pool sizing and saved-search alerts, not outreach: precision is too low, and the OR-chain pulls in space-enthusiast and hobbyist noise that inflates the count.
B · INTERMEDIATE (core weekly block)
Est. ~80-120 against target archetypes
("Propulsion Engineer" OR "Propulsion Systems Engineer" OR "Rocket Propulsion Engineer" OR "Propulsion Test Engineer") AND (thermofluids OR combustion OR "hot-fire" OR "thrust stand") AND ([TARGET_CO_1] OR [TARGET_CO_2] OR [TARGET_CO_3] OR [TARGET_CO_4]) NOT (intern OR recruiter OR "talent acquisition")
Filters: current or most recent employer is a Tier S/A or Tier A/B archetype target · LA, Denver, or Houston metro
Use for: the core weekly sourcing block. Pair with a live trigger from artifact 03: same string, very different reply rate.
C · ADVANCED (passive, senior)
Est. ~15-25 nationally
(Senior OR Staff OR Principal OR Lead) AND ("Propulsion Engineer" OR "Propulsion Systems Engineer") AND ("hot-fire" OR "thrust stand" OR "feed system" OR propellant OR CFD) NOT ("open to work" OR recruiter OR hiring)
Filters: 8+ years · the "open to work" exclusion is deliberate: it reaches true passives
Use for: researched, personalized outreach only. A templated message wastes the list, and the blind spot is a result set small enough that over-fishing it burns the pool for a quarter.
D · Peripheral pools: run this one
Highest hit rate per hour
("Powertrain Engineer" OR "Combustion Engineer" OR "Turbine Engineer" OR "Propulsion Engineer") AND (thermofluids OR combustion OR "high-pressure fluid systems") AND (automotive OR "gas turbine" OR motorsport OR "chemical process" OR "missile propulsion") NOT (sales OR intern OR "test technician")
Why it wins: the thermofluids and combustion vocabulary is the real skill proxy, and excluding sales and technician titles keeps the list to engineers who can ramp onto flight hardware.
Do this: tag every result in the ATS as PERIPHERAL and note the transfer strength (near-direct, strong, or medium) from artifact 03 so cross-industry conversion becomes measurable.
E · X-ray & community
Slow burn, high signal
site:linkedin.com/in ("propulsion engineer" OR "propulsion systems") "hot-fire" ("thrust stand" OR "feed system") ("[METRO_1]" OR "[METRO_2]") -recruiter site:github.com ("liquid rocket" OR "hybrid rocket" OR [OSS_FRAMEWORK_1]) ("CFD" OR "combustion model") followers:>15 site:github.com (rocketry OR "propulsion team") university "thrust stand" location:"[METRO_1]" ([CONFERENCE_1] OR [CONFERENCE_2] OR "AIAA Propulsion and Energy") speaker 2025 2026 propulsion
Why bother: open-source propulsion projects and university rocket-team contributors are self-selected talent who chose this problem unpaid. A propulsion conference speaker list is usually the single densest watering hole available, and almost nobody works it. Manual review only: no automated retrieval, no bulk export. Each platform's terms govern; check them before you run these.

04
Timing

Timing discipline for a scarce pool

The same message sent at the right moment in someone's year performs several times better. In a pool this small, timing is the cheapest lever available and the easiest one to waste.

Send-window rule

Tuesday to Thursday, 7:00-9:00am candidate-local.

Engineers check personal email before the test cell or shop floor gets loud. Avoid Friday afternoon entirely and Monday before 10.

Trigger response SLA

72 hours from event to first personalized touch.

Program cancellation, layoff notice, test anomaly, funding milestone. The trigger-tuned variant lives in artifact 05: the only variable left is reaction time.

Vest-cycle and relocation timing

Target 4-8 weeks before a vesting cliff. Avoid the two weeks after.

The weeks before a vesting decision are when people are most actively weighing options. Pair with the geography read in artifact 03: relocation willingness varies sharply by metro and local housing market; check the geography read in artifact 03 before assuming.

Working backward from one hire: 2 offers at an 82% offer-to-accept rate, 8 onsites at 25% onsite-to-offer, 22 screens at 37% screen-to-onsite. Every rate comes from the benchmark file: this team carries no house data yet for this pool.

The volume cost of a concentrated pool: those 22 screens need 550 contacts at the realistic 4% drought rate for this search, not the 367 a general 6% software-recruiting benchmark would predict. Budget the calendar for 550, not 367.

Caution

This pool is small and geographically concentrated: burning it with generic, mass-blasted outreach costs more than a slow start does. Personalize every touch to the specific hardware problem, and track re-contact carefully so the same names are not hit twice within a quarter.

07
Testing

A/B tests for a thin, technical pool

Run one test at a time, report weekly, keep the winner. This turns a scarce search into a repeatable capability instead of a one-off scramble, and it is the section most teams skip. This matrix names the frame to test and the metric to decide on; the deployed copy variants live in artifact 05.

Test Variant A Variant B Sample Decide on
Opening subject line Curiosity frame: opens a technical loop Identity frame: speaks to professional identity 50 / arm Reply rate
First-touch call to action Soft ask: low-commitment framing Direct ask: a named time, explicit call 50 / arm Positive reply
Value proposition · touch 3 Ownership-led: full subsystem, not a slice Stage-led: scope and ownership available at this stage 45 / arm Meeting booked
Persona routing Persona-matched copy to the adjacent-industry pool Generic copy to the same pool 30 / arm Reply rate
Send window Tuesday 7:30am local Thursday 4:00pm local 40 / arm Open + reply
Insight

Sample sizes here stay small: this pool does not support statistical elegance. Look for the difference between 10% and 25% reply, not a p-value. Kill any test that has not produced a decision within three weeks; a stalled test just burns pipeline.
