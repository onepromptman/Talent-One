01
The loop

Five stages, cheapest cut first

Ordered so the cheapest cut comes first. Nobody spends four hours on a thrust-stand problem to fail on something the thirty-minute screen would have surfaced.

30 min · recruiter
Recruiter screen
Motivation, comp expectations, logistics. Advance fast or pass fast.
45 min · HM
Hiring manager
Scope fit and ownership appetite. Sells the mission as hard as it screens.
60 min · propulsion eng
Technical screen
Thermofluids and analysis against a real problem. The can-they-do-it filter.
75 min · 2 eng
Technical deep-dive
Design depth, a thrust-stand anomaly, and the numbers. Highest-signal stage.

03
Questions

Questions with the bar written down

A question with no written bar is a vibe check. Each card says what a four sounds like and what a two sounds like, so two interviewers score the same answer the same way.

Tech 2 · propulsion test anomaly
20 min
"A hot-fire reads eight percent low on thrust, but chamber pressure looks nominal. Walk me through what you rule out, in order."
Scores 4
Separates measurement from physics: checks load-cell calibration and stand alignment before touching the engine. Asks what changed since the last good run. Names what would disprove each theory.
Scores 2
Blames the injector at once and redesigns it. Trusts the thrust number without checking the stand or the sensors. Cannot say what evidence would change their mind.
Tech 2 · feed-system design tradeoff
25 min
"Design a feed system that still delivers rated flow if one valve sticks shut. What do you cut to ship it in a week, and why is that cut safe?"
Scores 4
Names the failure mode first, offers two real architectures with a stated tradeoff, and says exactly what they cut under the week and why it stays safe for this failure.
Scores 2
Proposes one architecture with no fallback, cannot say what actually fails for the vehicle when the valve sticks, and needs the interviewer to name the tradeoff first.
HM · ownership on a small team
15 min
"Tell me about a propulsion problem you owned end to end that nobody assigned you. What made it worth the effort, and what did it cost?"
Scores 4
A specific gap they caught before anyone flagged it, the real cost of ignoring it, the actual sequence of what they did, and who they had to convince.
Scores 2
Describes assigned test or analysis work as if it were initiative. No cost named, no tradeoff, no other people in the story, and no reflection.
Sys · works with hardware partner
15 min
"Describe a time your analysis said the hardware was wrong and the partner disagreed. How did you settle it?"
Scores 4
Built test or analysis evidence before making the claim, treats the partner as competent, and reports what happened after, including where they turned out partly wrong.
Scores 2
The story is about being right. The hardware partner is the antagonist. No evidence step, and no sign they updated when new data arrived.
04
Debrief

The debrief, and the two hard-noes

Scores are written before the room opens. The order exists to stop the most senior or the loudest voice from anchoring everyone else.

Run it in this order
1
Scores written and submitted first
No score, no vote. Whoever has not written their scorecard does not speak first.
2
Lowest scorer opens the room
Inverts seniority on purpose, so concerns get air before the loudest enthusiasm anchors everyone.
3
Owners read one row each
Follow the heat grid. A three on a hard senior question is not a three on an easy one.
4
Calibrate, do not average
Weight for hawk-or-dove interviewers, early-round nerves, and format mismatch before comparing scores.
5
Decide inside the hour
Any single one forces extended discussion. Then hire, no-hire, or one named missing check with an owner.
Gate: hard no

Cannot reason about a propulsion system as one coupled thermal, fluid, and structural problem. After two stages of prompting they still size a component in isolation. On a small team shipping flight hardware, that is the expensive miss.

Gate: hard no

Treats the hardware partner as an obstacle rather than a peer, and cannot name a test result that once changed their mind. Unworkable on a team this small.

Not a gate

No launch-prime pedigree. No master's degree. No one specific analysis package. None of these predict propulsion judgment, and every one of them shrinks an already small pool.
