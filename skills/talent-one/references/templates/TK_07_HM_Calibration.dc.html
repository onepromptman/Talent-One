Timing

Why the demand curve favors moving now

These are structural, publicly disclosed forces, not a rumored competitor event. Demand for propulsion talent is climbing across three fronts while the qualified pool stays small, which is the whole argument for moving fast on the SLA.

Window · New Space launch demand

Commercial space has added tens of thousands of jobs across launch, constellations, and defense. Demand for propulsion engineers is outpacing a pool that grows slowly, so every credible candidate is being courted by several employers at once (industry 2025-26).

Window · new propulsion frontiers

Hypersonics, long-endurance drones, and nuclear space propulsion are pulling on the same talent, while green propellant and electric propulsion are the hot specialties. New programs open faster than schools graduate propulsion engineers (industry 2026).

Your edge · mid-career shortage

The scarcity concentrates at 5-15 years with real flight-program experience, which is exactly the band this search targets. A small team offering hands-on hardware ownership is a genuine differentiator for that engineer. Move while that pitch is fresh (industry 2025).

E
Question bank

Ask these, in this order

Each question extracts one thing you cannot source without. The note says what it pulls out and what a bad answer tells you: a vague answer here reliably predicts a vague propulsion search.

"What does success look like in the first 90 days, and what will this engineer have designed, analyzed, or tested by the end of year one?"
Extracts  Concrete scope for the posting and the interview scenarios: which hardware, which tests, which analysis. A vague answer here predicts a vague search, so push twice before moving on.
"Which component or subsystem will they own outright on day one: feed system, thrust chamber, test stand? Who owns it today?"
Extracts  Ownership scope, plus whether this is a backfill or growth seat. Candidates ask this on the first call, so you need the answer before they do.
"Who was your last great propulsion hire, and what specifically made them exceptional in their first six months?"
Extracts  The real bar, in behavioral terms. Converts "strong engineer" into observable evidence you can screen for.
"Walk me through the team: who do they report to, how do they work with external suppliers, will this role ever have direct reports?"
Extracts  Structure for the posting and the candidate FAQ. Also surfaces the supplier-coordination load before a candidate discovers it at onsite.
"What is the hardest propulsion problem the team is staring at this quarter: combustion instability, thermal margin, a hot-fire anomaly?"
Extracts  Interview deep-dive material and the single best outreach hook for experienced engineers. Get it verbatim: paraphrasing kills it.
"A candidate is excellent in liquid propulsion but has only dabbled in electric. Screen out, or screen in and train?"
Extracts  Whether the propulsion-type requirement is real or inherited. Confirm the answer still holds when you present an actual candidate.
"They have deep thermofluids and test experience from gas turbines, automotive powertrain, or motorsport, but no space heritage. Yes or no?"
Extracts  The adjacent-industry boundary. This one answer roughly doubles or halves the reachable pool.
"Which requirement would you drop to close two weeks faster?"
Extracts  Any answer is useful. "None" means the timeline is not real, and you should say so in the room rather than discovering it in week seven.
"What would make you pass on an otherwise technically excellent propulsion candidate?"
Extracts  Real disqualifiers, and often an unstated bias you can address now rather than at debrief.
"What is the unglamorous part of this job you would rather I not mention: night test-stand shifts, supplier travel, qualification paperwork?"
Extracts  The cause of your late-stage dropouts. Put it in the posting instead: naming it raises reply quality and kills the offer-stage surprise.
"Give me your sixty-second pitch for this role: impact, hardest challenge, why now."
Extracts  Authentic selling language in their voice. Record it. It goes straight into outreach and the screen close.
"Can you commit to scorecard feedback within two business days, and a named panel this week?"
Extracts  The SLA. In a scarce, concentrated pool, hiring-manager latency is the single largest controllable loss factor. Ask for the commitment out loud.

Requirement calibration

Each documented requirement, checked against a small, concentrated propulsion pool. Bring this table to the meeting already filled in with your recommendation: it is far easier to get a verdict on a proposal than on an open question.

Requirement, as documented Verdict Market check & recommendation
Propulsion system design (liquid, solid, hybrid, or electric) Must-have Hold. This is the core signal and the deep-dive is built to verify it. Defining the search around it, not one specific type, keeps the small pool open.
Thermofluids: thermodynamics, fluid dynamics, heat transfer Must-have Hold. The physics under every propulsion decision, and cheap to verify at screen. No pool cost: any real candidate has it.
Combustion & propellant chemistry, or electric-propulsion physics Calibrated Hold, but read it as either/or across propulsion types. Requiring one specific chemistry narrows an already small pool sharply for little gain.
Propulsion test: hot-fire / thrust-stand, instrumentation, data reduction Calibrated Hold as must-have, but accept adjacent test-rig experience: engine dyno, turbine test cells, high-pressure fluid rigs. This is where the peripheral pools open up.
CAD plus analysis: CFD, FEA, thermal (ANSYS, NX, SolidWorks) Flex Require the analysis skill, not the named package. A strong analyst learns a new tool in weeks; screening on the specific suite cuts the funnel for no real gain.
Systems engineering on flight hardware: requirements, tolerances, verification Flex Flight heritage specifically is scarce. Accept rigorous hardware-qualification experience from gas turbine, automotive powertrain, motorsport, or defense, and ramp on the space environment.
High-temperature materials / additive manufacturing Nice-to-have Tiebreaker only. Valuable for chamber and nozzle work, but rare enough that requiring it would empty the pool. Make sure sourcers are not hard-filtering on it.
Feed-system / valve design; MATLAB or Python; AS9100 process Nice-to-have All trainable on the job. Treat as a plus that speeds ramp, never a filter. Process fluency in particular is learned faster than propulsion physics.

The trade-off lock and a mutual SLA

Every search optimizes two of three. Make the choice explicit and record it, then commit both sides in writing. Send this as the meeting recap the same day: a propulsion search that slips almost always slips on the right-hand column.

Pick two: speed · bar · comp
Speed + Bar
Comp lands at the top of the band. Approve the ceiling up front, not at offer, or the finalist walks to a better-funded program.
Speed + Comp
The bar flexes on space heritage. Hire strong thermofluids and test talent from an adjacent industry, and commit to a real ramp plan.
Bar + Comp
Timeline extends past the market average. Each extra month of vacancy is a month of design and hot-fire work not getting done. Sometimes correct, but say it out loud.
