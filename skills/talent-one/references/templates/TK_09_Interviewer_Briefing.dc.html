Interviewer Briefing

Battery Systems Engineer Four stages briefed · one debrief form · one decision meeting

A briefing that repeats the whole interview plan wastes the ten minutes an interviewer actually has. Give each one their own stage, the specific thing to probe, and the two lines they are not allowed to ask.

Briefing budget
Interviewers briefed4
Prep time each10 min
Debrief window24 hours
Decision meeting30 min
One page per interviewer. Nothing more to read.
01
Prep

Your stage, not the whole plan

Each interviewer gets the ten-minute version of the plan: what they own, what to probe, and what to review before the candidate joins.

45 min · HM
Hiring manager
Scope fit and ownership appetite. Read the tradeoff lock before the room opens.
60 min · battery eng
Technical screen
The can-they-do-it filter. Bring the two backup questions, not just the primary.
75 min · 2 eng
Technical deep-dive
Design depth and a test-rig anomaly. Split ownership before the loop, not during it.
45 min · cross-team
Systems & values
Someone outside the reporting line. Score the scenario, not the resume.
02
Coaching

What every interviewer holds before the room

Stated once here so it never has to be repeated per stage. Read it every time, not only the first time.

Score the answer, not the accent
A different communication style or a nontraditional path is not a competency gap. Write down the words, not the delivery.
One story is not a pattern
A single strong or weak answer sets that competency's own score, never the rest of the loop. Halo and horn effects are real.
Never ask about protected status
Family plans, age, national origin, disability, and religion stay off the table, directly or by inference from a resume gap.
Write the example, not the adjective
"Strong" is not evidence a debrief can use. The specific thing they said or did is the only entry worth writing down.
Caution

An interviewer who skips the ten-minute prep window shows up reading the resume live, in front of the candidate. That reads as disorganized and it costs the loop a stage's worth of signal, so protect the window on the calendar.

03
Debrief form

Four fields, the same form every time

A rating with no cited evidence is a vibe with a number attached. Every field below asks for the specific thing, never the summary judgment.

Rating 1 to 4
Technical depth
Cite the exchange that earned this score. An adjective alone is not a valid entry.
Rating 1 to 4
Independence
Name the hint you had to give, or say they reached it alone.
Yes or no
Deal-breaker triggered
Cite the pattern the plan named, or leave this one blank.
Free text
One line for the room
The sentence you would say out loud before anyone averages a score.
04
Decision

The decision meeting, in order

Thirty minutes, same order every time, so the loudest voice in the room never sets the outcome by going first.

Run it in this order
1
Scores in before the room opens
No score, no vote. Whoever has not submitted a form does not speak first.
2
Lowest scorer speaks first
Seniority does not open the meeting. Concerns get air before enthusiasm anchors the room.
3
Each owner reads their form
Follow the stage order from the interview plan, not seniority or volume.
4
Decide inside the thirty minutes
Hire, no-hire, or one named missing check with an owner and a date.
Legal line

Every candidate for this role gets the same stages, the same questions, and the same rubric. A deviation made for one candidate and not another is the fact pattern that turns a rejection into a claim.
